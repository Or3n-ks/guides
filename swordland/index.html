Swordland Showdown

Operating rule

Own buildings, not just roles.

Each priority building has one named owner responsible for capture, garrison, refills, and any handoff. The mobile strike group adds force to the commander's chosen target. Hold until the next garrison is confirmed, unless command orders an evacuation with a destination and collection plan.

Before entry: Pin buildings by name, fill in player names for the roster below, and select a deputy caller. “Middle left” is too ambiguous during a fight. Pick garrison leaders and a rally leader for their combat performance, not total account power alone.

Opponent branches

Choose from actual battle performance.

Use the shared opening below. Once battle reports and garrisons show what each side can hold, the commander picks a branch and calls any change in target.

+

=

Protect two anchors, use one mobile strike, and flip the best banked target.

−

Avoid repeat losses to their best garrison. Farm reachable points and prepare one credible ambush.

Judge strength from live garrisons, reports, attendance, and refill speed, not the signup power label alone. Reassess at center unlock and with eight minutes left.

Safe zone or forward town

Move forward for a timed job.

A town in the safe zone can still send marches to buildings, rallies, drops, and Undercellars. Keep the town there unless a shorter route changes a capture, refill, rally, or collection window. Base that decision on the player's actual battle role and the opponent's live attacks, not total account power alone.

By player: A lower-power joiner can reinforce and gather from safety; do not teleport them forward just because their squad owns a building. A top rally or garrison player may also stay safe if their march reaches the objective on time. Forward placement requires an active player who can keep the town empty, extinguish fire, and move again when command changes target. An empty exposed town loses no troops when hit, but neglected fire can force it back to the safe zone and disrupt the planned response.

March queues and city position

Put troops to work in priority order.

For a building squad member, start with one strong march in the assigned building. Put a second march in a named nearby allied building if it needs troops. Keep one march available for a called rally or urgent refill; if the city must stay empty, a nearby tent can stage that reserve. Send the remaining marches to assigned buildings, point drops, or Undercellars as the battle allows.

A tent is a staging location, not an automatic reinforcement: it does not add troops to the building garrison or earn occupation points. Do not reserve one tent per player if those troops could usefully defend another building. The exact recall and redeploy time from an encampment was not confirmed in the available Swordland rules, so time one in your next match before relying on a last-second tent response.

  • 1Best march: assigned building, rally lead, or called attack. Do not weaken an anchor for a gathering node.
  • 2Next marches: refill the same garrison, cover a nearby secondary building, or join the called rally with its requested first-slot hero and troops.
  • 3Truly spare marches: collect dropped supplies and gather Undercellars. Recall them when a priority building or rally needs the queue or troops.

For an attack rally, Chenko or Yeonwoo in the first hero slot is a useful default for joiners. For a defensive refill, Gordon is one useful first-slot option. The building owner may request a different mix based on the active garrison skills and battle report. A weak or irrelevant first-slot skill can displace a better shared skill.

Empty cities: A hit on an empty city injures no troops. Forward players can deploy their troops and extinguish fires promptly; the 100-gem extinguish price is player-observed, so check the live prompt. Neglected fire drains Guard Station health and can force a move. Safe-zone cities can also send marches. Leaving the battlefield for a full heal is different and triggers a 12-minute return wait.

Incoming attack

Defend a contestable building. Evacuate a certain loss.

A powerful attacker alone is not enough reason to leave. The owner reports the incoming march or rally, estimated arrival, our garrison, the building's visible relic bank, and whether reinforcements can land in time. Command then calls DEFEND or EVAC before impact.

Evacuation saves immediate fighting capacity, not the building's relic bank: if the enemy takes control, stored points can drop for either side to collect. It also gives up occupation income and may start a new capture timer for a retake. Check the live battle report and score. Troop injuries can be healed; leaving Swordland for a full heal imposes a 12-minute return wait.

Enemy buildings

Attack for a score change, not activity.

  • 1Select: command names one target because its garrison is beatable, its buff matters, or its visible relic bank could close the gap.
  • 2Build: strike leader rallies a defended building. Utility squads lend spare joiners; Sanctum owners approve any march leaving an anchor. Solo only if empty or clearly beatable.
  • 3Land: name the garrison leader and reinforcement players before launch. A win without a hold plan hands the building back.
  • 4Collect: coordinators position nearby spare marches before impact. Either side can pick up dropped points.
  • 5Decide: call TAKEN—FILL, HELD, or STOP. Read the report before another failed rally.

Full control normally follows a three-minute occupation timer; Bell Tower shortens it. Starting a one-minute rally with two minutes left is a risky loot attempt, not a reliable full-control plan. A displayed 20,000-point bank is accumulated relic score, not a fixed bonus. If the opponent loses that bank and your side collects it all, the apparent score swing could approach 40,000; actual timing and collection decide the result.
